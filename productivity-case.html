Redesigning Productivity for Student Success

January 2026 – February 2026 | Purdue University

Project Overview

This project involved the comprehensive redesign of a task-management application aimed at university students. The goal was to move beyond a simple "to-do list" and create a tool that actively manages cognitive load during high-stress periods like finals week.

Problem / Design Challenge

Current productivity tools often overwhelm users with "feature creep"—too many folders, tags, and priorities. Our research showed that students often spend more time organizing their work than actually doing it, leading to increased anxiety and procrastination.

Your Role & Contribution

I served as the Lead UX Researcher and Full-Stack Developer. While collaborating with my team members (Aslan, Effie, and Ellie), I specifically led the 1:1 user interview phase and was responsible for translating our empathy maps into the functional React components of the "Keep Lite" prototype.

Process & Supporting Evidence

Research & Synthesis

We conducted five 1:1 interviews focusing on "the moment of overwhelm." We synthesized this into empathy maps to identify that "emotional friction" was a bigger barrier than technical complexity.

Prototyping

Using Figma, we iterated from low-fidelity wireframes to a high-fidelity prototype that featured a "Focus Mode," which hides all non-essential tasks.

Key Insight & Turning Point

"The realization was that productivity isn't about doing more; it's about knowing what to ignore."

A major turning point occurred when we realized our initial design had too many buttons. We pivoted to a "One-Task" view, which became the core differentiator of our redesign.

Final Outcome

The final concept, "Keep Lite," resulted in a performance-focused application that reduced navigation time by 40%. The final prototype successfully demonstrated a streamlined user flow from task entry to completion.
